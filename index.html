About

I work in the gap between the roadmap and the people expected to use it.

That gap is where most transformation stalls, so that’s where I spend my time. I go on-site, map how operations actually run, rank where technology will pay off, and stay through rollout until the new way of working sticks.

At Meridian Data Labs, that meant bringing AI and industrial IoT to the Indian Navy, DRDO, HAL, GRSE and the Indian Coast Guard, in facilities with strict MoD compliance and no internet. Building the platform was the smaller part of the job. Getting it trusted and used was the larger part. For a utility that came to us with an open brief, it meant mapping their operations into 15 AI use cases and anchoring the roadmap on one high-value win instead of fifteen pilots.

Before that I took Optiply, a Dutch inventory and purchasing SaaS, into India with no team, no customers and no playbook. Two years later we had 15 people and a US$100k ARR pipeline, and I’d owned the ERP integrations through go-live myself.

I trained as a computer engineer across hardware and software, with a minor in product design, and I still build: agents, automations, integrations and infrastructure. That keeps my roadmaps honest. I’m now completing the MBET at the University of Waterloo, specializing in AI & Digital Transformation, and I’m a teaching assistant for its Digital Leadership & Transformation course.

How I work

Habits I picked up deploying where nobody could help.

  1. Sit with the work first

    I watch before I build. At Optiply and at MDL, the real workflow was different from the documented one almost every time.

  2. One win before fifteen pilots

    Rank every idea by value and ease, then put the effort behind the strongest one. Momentum sells the rest of the roadmap.

  3. Own the adoption

    A pilot nobody uses is a cost. I stay close to users through testing and rollout, because getting a system trusted is harder than building it.

  4. Make the tenth deploy boring

    Automate it, runbook it, and make rollback one command. If every rollout needs a hero, the system isn’t done.

  5. Write it down

    Integration guides, runbooks, prompt playbooks. Each one is written so the next person can use it without getting me on a call.
